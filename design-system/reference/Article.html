Choosing a boring queue

Why the most reliable message broker we ran was the database we already had.

4 Nov 20268 min readPostgres · Go

For four years our background jobs ran on a broker we had written ourselves. It was fast, clever, and understood by two people. When one of them left, it became the riskiest system we owned.

Note

Latency figures are p99 over 30 days of production traffic.

The replacement had to be dull: something every engineer already knew how to query, back up and page on. Postgres, with FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED, turned out to be enough.

SELECT id, payload
FROM jobs
WHERE run_at <= now() AND locked_at IS NULL
ORDER BY run_at
LIMIT 32
FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED;

What we gave up

Peak throughput is lower than the old broker's synthetic benchmark. In practice we never came within a factor of ten of either ceiling, and we deleted 9,000 lines of code.

The broker is gone. Nobody has asked for it back.
